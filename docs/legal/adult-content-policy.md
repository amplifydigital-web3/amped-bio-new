# 1. Position in brief

1.  Amped.bio is a platform and storefront system. Creators sell. Fans
    buy from creators.

2.  The creator is the seller of record. The sale is a contract between
    the fan and the creator. Amped is not a party to it, except to run
    the checkout, collect its platform fee and route the creator's
    share.

3.  The creator is the producer of the content and keeps the records the
    law requires. Amped does not produce, commission, select, edit,
    rank, recommend or promote 18+ content.

4.  Amped holds the gates it controls: who may sell 18+, who may view
    18+, what may be published, and how fast it comes down.

5.  We adopt the established model used by OnlyFans, Fanvue and Fansly.
    We do not invent new structures. Where we go further than those
    platforms, it is because the payment rail or the law requires it.

# 3. Where neutrality holds, and where it does not

Neutral territory is the right position for the sale and for production.
It does not reach the gates. Counsel should read the policy with this
boundary in mind.

## 3.1 Where it holds

- **Seller of record.** The fan buys from the creator. The receipt names
  the creator as seller. Amped is seller only of its own items, such as
  paid themes, and never of 18+ items.

- **Producer status.** The creator films, photographs or creates the
  content. Amped hosts and delivers it.

- **Ownership.** The creator owns the content and grants Amped a licence
  to host and deliver it.

- **Creator tax.** The creator reports and pays tax on creator earnings.

## 3.2 Where it does not hold

- **State age verification laws** reach the commercial entity that
  publishes or distributes the material, and several reach hosts and
  platforms directly. Kansas covers a person who knowingly hosts the
  site. Arizona and Missouri expressly include social media platforms.
  Iowa reaches sites, apps and portions of them. South Dakota reaches
  anyone who in the regular course of business hosts or makes such
  material available. Amped runs the page, the checkout and the
  delivery. We treat Amped as responsible for viewer age assurance.

- **TAKE IT DOWN Act.** Amped is a covered platform. The notice and
  removal duty is Amped's.

- **CSAM reporting.** Amped is a provider under 18 U.S.C. 2258A. The
  reporting duty is Amped's.

- **Trafficking and FOSTA.** Federal criminal law is not displaced by
  Section 230. Amped prohibits and polices offers of in-person sexual
  services.

OnlyFans carries the same duties while keeping the creator as seller.
That is the model. Neutral on the sale, responsible for the gates.

# 4. Adult Content Policy

This section is the operative policy text. It will be published as the
Amped.bio Adult Content Policy and incorporated into the Creator Terms.

## 4.1 Scope

This Policy applies to any content that shows nudity or actual or
simulated sexually explicit conduct ("18+ content"). It applies on every
surface: bio pages, the store, memberships, messages, Broadcast, live
streams and the Fan Library.

## 4.2 Who may sell 18+ content

- The creator is at least 18 and passes the Authbase Over 18 check.

- The creator completes RNS verification before collecting USDC.

- The creator accepts the Creator Terms, this Policy and the Contract
  between Fan and Creator.

- The creator designates a custodian of records for 2257 purposes in 18+
  settings.

- The creator turns on 18+ in settings. 18+ is off by default.

- An agency may manage a creator's account. The creator remains
  responsible for every item on it. An agency that films or produces
  content is itself a producer of that content.

## 4.3 People who appear

- Every person who appears in 18+ content passes the Authbase Over 18
  check and signs a release in the app before the item can be submitted.

- The release is between the creator and that person. Amped is not a
  party to it.

- A person who appears may withdraw consent. On withdrawal, the item
  comes down.

- No person may appear who has not been checked. Hiding a face does not
  remove the requirement.

## 4.4 Labels and placement

- The creator labels every 18+ item. Unlabelling a reviewed item sends
  it back to review.

- AI generated or AI altered content carries an AI label.

- 18+ items appear only behind the age gate: the creator's 18+ shelf,
  store and membership area, and the fan's own Library.

- 18+ items never appear on the public bio preview, in Explore, search,
  recommendations, email, push previews, share cards or link previews.

## 4.5 Review before publish

- Every 18+ item and every new version passes the classifier and a human
  reviewer before it publishes.

- Review has two outcomes: approve as submitted, or remove under this
  Policy. Reviewers do not edit, crop, caption, retitle, bundle, reorder
  or feature content.

- Technical formatting for delivery, such as transcoding and resizing,
  is not editing.

## 4.6 Prohibited content

The following is prohibited in every case. The list follows the OnlyFans
Acceptable Use Policy and the baseline that payment and infrastructure
providers apply across the market.

- Anyone under 18, or content that presents a person as under 18: age
  play, school settings, youth costumes, or words that suggest a minor.

- Any person who has not passed the check and signed a release.

- Non-consent, real or simulated, including sleep, intoxication or
  hypnosis.

- Incest, including roleplay.

- Bestiality and necrophilia.

- Extreme violence, blood, serious bodily harm, or weapons in a sexual
  context.

- Hidden camera, upskirt or voyeur content, and any recording of a
  person without their consent.

- A digital forgery or deepfake of a real person without that person's
  consent.

- Explicit conduct in a place where the public is likely to see it.

- Offers of in-person meetings, escort services, or sex for payment.

- Signs of coercion or trafficking.

- Drug use in sexual content, and excrement.

- Links to outside storage or payment that route around the age gate or
  checkout.

- Content that is illegal where the creator or the fan is located.

## 4.7 Enforcement

- A removal for a policy reason is a strike. Three strikes in 12 months
  turns off 18+ for the creator.

- Child safety content, or a confirmed upload of a person without
  consent, suspends the account at once. Amped preserves the material
  and reports it as the law requires.

- Creator payouts settle in the same transaction as the sale, so Amped
  cannot claw back paid shares. Amped may pause the store and new sales
  during an investigation.

- Creators may appeal a removal or a strike. Child safety removals are
  final.

## 4.8 Reports and takedown

- Anyone may report content, with or without an account.

- A Take It Down request form is public and needs no account. A facially
  valid request hides the item at once. Amped decides within 48 hours.

- Amped removes identical copies by hash match and offers the requester
  a StopNCII hash share.

## 4.9 Records Amped keeps

- Check outcomes: pass or fail, time, and the Authbase reference. Amped
  does not store ID images.

- Release signatures and the creator's designated custodian of records.

- Review decisions, strikes, reports, takedowns and appeals, in an audit
  log.

- Material preserved for a CyberTipline report, for the period the law
  sets.

# 5. Age assurance by state

## 5.1 Our standard

One standard for the whole country. Every viewer of any 18+ item, in
every state, passes the Authbase Over 18 check before the item renders
or can be bought. There is no state by state switch for the United
States.

Three reasons. First, 27 states now require age verification for adult
content, and the count rises each session. Second, most statutes use a
one third or substantial portion test, and a creator's 18+ shelf, store
and membership area are dedicated adult surfaces. Iowa and others reach
portions of a site, so the sitewide share does not protect us. Third,
location is unreliable, and Utah's 2026 VPN provisions are in
litigation. A national gate removes the location question.

## 5.2 Method requirements we design to

Self declaration is accepted nowhere. We build to the strictest common
terms across the statutes.

# 6. 18 U.S.C. 2257 position

## 6.1 Position

Amped is not a primary or secondary producer of any 18+ content. The
creator is the primary producer of the content the creator films,
photographs or creates. The creator keeps the records that 18 U.S.C.
2257 and 28 CFR Part 75 require for every person who appears, and
designates a custodian of records. Amped requires this as a condition of
selling 18+ and can require the creator to produce the records.

## 6.2 Basis

- **Primary producer** is the person who actually films, photographs or
  creates the depiction (28 CFR 75.1(c)(1)). That is the creator.

- **Secondary producer** includes a person who publishes or reproduces
  the material, inserts a digital image on a computer site, or otherwise
  manages the sexually explicit content of a computer site (75.1(c)(2)).
  To manage content means to make editorial or managerial decisions
  about the sexually explicit content (75.1(k)).

- **The hosting exclusion.** Producer does not include a person whose
  activity is limited to transmission, storage, retrieval, hosting,
  formatting or translation of a communication without selection or
  alteration of its content. Deleting material in a manner consistent
  with 47 U.S.C. 230(c) is not selection or alteration (75.1(c)(4)(v)).

- **The performer exclusion.** Producer does not include activity that
  does not involve hiring, contracting for, managing or otherwise
  arranging for the participation of the performers (75.1(c)(4)(iii)).

## 6.3 The questions counsel must answer

1.  **Pre-publication review.** Amped approves or removes every 18+ item
    before it publishes. Our view: an approve or remove decision under a
    published policy is a Section 230(c) removal decision, which the
    rule says is not selection. Amped never chooses which item runs,
    where it runs, or in what order. The risk is that a reviewer
    approval reads as an editorial decision under 75.1(k). Counsel to
    confirm.

2.  **The in-app release.** Amped supplies the release form and the
    check. If Amped were the counterparty, it could look like arranging
    for the participation of performers. We draft the release as the
    creator's document, between the creator and the person who appears,
    with Amped as neither party nor beneficiary. Counsel to approve the
    text.

3.  **The platform fee.** Amped takes 5% of each sale. The hosting
    exclusion in 75.1(c)(4)(v) has no condition about commercial
    interest. Only the processing exclusion in 75.1(c)(4)(i) does. We do
    not see the fee as a problem. Counsel to confirm.

## 6.4 Enforcement climate

In Free Speech Coalition v. Attorney General, 974 F.3d 408 (3d Cir.
2020), the Third Circuit limited 2257 on First Amendment grounds. The
Free Speech Coalition reads the decision as assuming the secondary
producer requirements are unconstitutional without ruling on them,
because no party was only a secondary producer. The decision is
precedential in the Third Circuit only. We do not rely on it. We rely on
the exclusions and the operating rules below.

## 6.5 Operating rules that keep the position

- Amped never produces, commissions, pays for or arranges 18+ content.

- Amped never edits, crops, captions, retitles, bundles, reorders or
  watermarks 18+ content. Technical formatting for delivery is allowed.

- Amped never features, ranks, recommends, emails or promotes 18+ items,
  and never uses 18+ imagery in its own marketing.

- Review decisions are approve or remove only, recorded against the
  published Policy.

- The release is the creator's document.

A breach of any of these rules changes the analysis. Any feature that
touches 18+ placement or presentation goes to counsel before build.

## 6.6 Fallback if counsel disagrees

If counsel concludes Amped is a secondary producer, Amped names a
custodian of records, obtains from each creator the records or the
creator's custodian details, and shows the 2257 statement on every page
with 18+ content. That route means holding copies of ID, which conflicts
with our no ID retention design. Decide before go live, not after.

# 7. Other platform duties

## 7.1 TAKE IT DOWN Act

Amped is a covered platform: it serves the public and primarily provides
a forum for user content. Since 19 May 2026, a covered platform must run
a clear and conspicuous notice and removal process, remove nonconsensual
intimate imagery within 48 hours of a valid request, and make reasonable
efforts to remove identical copies. Good faith removal is protected even
if the content later proves lawful. The FTC enforces it at up to
\$53,088 per violation and accepts complaints at TakeItDown.ftc.gov. The
Act covers AI forgeries as well as real images. Section 4.8 implements
it. Amped keeps a log of intake, removal times and copy searches.

## 7.2 CSAM reporting

Amped is a provider under 18 U.S.C. 2258A and reports apparent child
sexual abuse material to the NCMEC CyberTipline as soon as reasonably
possible after actual knowledge. The REPORT Act (2024) adds child sex
trafficking under 18 U.S.C. 1591 and enticement of a minor under 18
U.S.C. 2422(b), and extends preservation from 90 days to one year. Amped
registers with NCMEC before go live and scans uploads with the
Cloudflare CSAM Scanning Tool.

## 7.3 Trafficking and in-person services

Section 4.6 prohibits offers of in-person sexual services and signs of
coercion. Reviewers escalate trafficking indicators to the safety lead,
who reports as the law requires.

## 7.4 Copyright

Amped keeps a registered DMCA agent and its existing notice and counter
notice process.

## 7.5 Section 230

Section 230 protects Amped against most claims over third party content.
It does not cover federal criminal law or intellectual property. Some
age verification statutes exempt neutral hosts and search engines.
Counsel to confirm whether any exemption reaches Amped. We assume none
does and gate anyway.
